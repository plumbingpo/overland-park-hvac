A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us right away.

How Kansas City Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Most mid-season breakdowns start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Take a look at the age of your system. Its age says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and is also showing rising bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install in peak season.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
