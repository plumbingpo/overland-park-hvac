How We Work

Ductless Mini Split Installation, Done Properly

Good ductless comfort starts with a load calculation, not a catalog. We size each zone to what that room actually needs, since an oversized head short-cycles, leaves the air clammy, and never quite holds temperature. When one outdoor unit serves several heads, the engineering gets stricter: capacity has to be split sensibly, and line set length and elevation change must stay inside the manufacturer's tables or capacity quietly suffers. Style matters too, because a wall head, a ceiling cassette, and a low floor unit each change both airflow and the look of the room. Our experienced technicians review every room in person, agree on placements that work physically and visually, and plan line set routes that stay hidden where possible and neat where they show.

Our installation process is held to the same standard as our biggest central systems. Nitrogen goes into the line set first to prove it holds pressure, then a vacuum pump pulls it down to a level confirmed with a micron gauge. Flares are torqued and leak-tested one by one, since that joint is the classic failure point. Drainage is set up with real fall or a quiet condensate pump, and the refrigerant charge is adjusted to match the line length. Finally, every zone is commissioned in both modes and we show you how the remote actually works. Done Right

The Benefits of Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion creates a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing room-temperature air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors tolerate poorly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall stains a wall the first humid week.

Sizing is tighter with ductless than with central air. The smallest heads on the market are already more than many bedrooms need, so picking one size too big means short cycles, sticky humidity, and a room that never settles. That is why our experienced technicians size every zone honestly, then back it up with careful install work: each flare torqued and leak-tested, the circuit pressure-tested and evacuated to a measured vacuum, charge adjusted for the line length, and every head commissioned in heating and cooling. Handling refrigerant is federally regulated, and the unit needs a correctly sized circuit and disconnect. Done this way, a mini split runs quiet and efficient for fifteen years with nothing to report.

How to Prevent Ductless Mini Split Installation Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • Schedule service once a year so charge, flares, and drainage get verified before small issues grow
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
  • Have the indoor blower wheel and coil professionally cleaned before they grow anything
